Colour and width

Four axes over the ground, all of them in Oklab and each holding the other three still. Chroma scales the colour without touching the lightness, so turning it down makes the magenta quieter rather than the mark fainter. Hue turns the ground to another colour at the same strength, which is the other answer to a magenta that reads too pink. Lightness scales L on its own. Contrast spreads L about the ground's own mean, so the field's falloff steepens or flattens and 1.00 is a true no-op rather than an approximate one. Every axis at rest is what the files ship at, and the artwork itself already carries three grades of its own.

Ground picks which artwork the whole page is drawn from. Light is the mark — the file the site serves as its icon. Dark is the dark theme's own flare, rendered from the shader rather than recoloured from the light one, because the light ground never came off that shader in the first place. So the two are a pair by theme rather than the same gradient twice, and the dark one is an asset to reach for rather than something a tab switches to: it is not linked as an icon anywhere.

Width runs over the swash. The exported outline is read back as the brush stroke it is — the two sides paired off along it, and the line through the middle taken as its spine — and the control moves each side toward or away from that spine. So the curve keeps its path, its reach and its taper, and only its body thins or fills out. The outline stays curves rather than a traced polygon, and at 1.00 it is the path in the file, untouched. It reaches the mark and the avatars; the headers and the cards carry the ground alone.

The controls need JavaScript. The files linked below are the artwork as it ships.

Headers

The wide artwork, at each platform's own stated pixel size so an upload is never resampled on the way in. The lockup can go left, middle or right, in either ink, with or without the line underneath. Left is the safer default on all three platforms only if the artwork is not cropped; every one of them parks the avatar in the bottom-left corner, so on a short banner the right-hand setting is the one that stays clear of it. Middle centres the lockup in the frame, which reads on the wide masters and is the first thing a crop takes.

Repository cards

GitHub's social preview: the image that stands in for a repo wherever its link is posted. 1280 x 640, the size GitHub asks for. Name it, give it a line underneath if it wants one, and take the PNG — the artwork and the type are the same as the headers, so a repo reads as ours at a glance.

Colour

The site is set in ink on paper and reverses wholesale for the dark theme. The magenta belongs to the artwork; it is not an interface colour and nothing is set in it.

Type

Two families, both self-hosted. Instrument Serif carries the name and the headings; everything else is Barlow Semi Condensed, with Barlow's regular width reserved for names that are not ours.

  • Instrument Serif Regular The wordmark and every heading. 400 only.
  • Barlow Semi Condensed Regular Interface, navigation and body copy. 400 and 500.
  • Barlow Semi Condensed ExtraLight Italic The line under the wordmark, and nothing else.

Experimental

Sketches, not marks. Nothing here is in use and none of it replaces the artwork above — the section exists so the alternatives can be looked at beside it rather than described. Each one is drawn from the function rather than from the artwork: the plot itself, its level curves in the complex plane, and its polar form, all in the same 800-unit square the mark uses. The geometry is computed in the page, so a file taken from here is the curve and not a picture of it. k is the index the plots are tabulated over: on the real plots it sets how steep the curve is, on the complex ones how many levels are drawn.
